## MACRO WEEKLY BRIEFING — 2026-09-12

### 1. RATE & MONETARY POLICY
The Fed is firmly on hold with the target range at 3.50%–3.75% (effective 3.63%); the real 10Y yield sits near 2.6% (4.95% nominal minus 2.36% breakeven), a clearly restrictive stance. Qualitative communications reinforce the hold: Powell explicitly framed rates as being in a "good place" and signaled the Committee could "wait and see" how the Iran war drives oil and inflation before acting, while the Committee continues to "carefully assess incoming data" on any further adjustment. With headline inflation re-accelerating, we expect no cuts in the near term and a hawkish-hued September meeting.

### 2. INFLATION PICTURE
The disinflation path has stalled and is re-accelerating. Headline CPI rose to +3.71% YoY (Aug), core CPI +2.76%, while the Fed's preferred core PCE sits at +3.34% (July) — well above the 2% target. The pipeline is under fresh pressure: PPI final demand is +5.44% YoY, PPI core +4.62%, and PPI all-commodities jumped +9.85%, signaling CPI momentum will stay firm for 1–3 months. Breakevens remain anchored (5Y 2.40%, 10Y 2.36%) and 10Y breakeven stays in the 1.5–2.5% credibility band, but the oil shock from the Iran war is the swing factor feeding straight into the goods and transport complex.

### 3. LABOR MARKET
The labor market is holding up but cooling. Unemployment is 4.1% (down from 4.4% a year ago), with +162K payrolls in August and the openings/unemployed ratio at 1.03x (still tight). Initial claims are low at 206K. Wage pressure is contained but not disinflationary: average hourly earnings +3.09% YoY and the ECI at +3.14% — both below the ~3.5% threshold that would add service inflation, meaning wage-cost push is not the current inflationary driver; energy is.

### 4. GROWTH & GDP
Growth has clearly downshifted after the volatile 2025 swing. Q1 2026 real GDP growth is +1.5% QoQ annualized (down from +2.1% and +4.4% earlier), with YoY +2.10% ($24.27T real, $32.49T nominal). Real PCE is its main support (+3.4% QoQ). Manufacturing IP is expanding (+1.27% YoY), but the economic is running well below the 2025-26 estimates, and the M2 velocity uptick (1.42) signals money is circulating as nominal activity slows relative to money growth.

### 5. CREDIT & FINANCIAL CONDITIONS
Financial conditions are remarkably benign despite the rate stance. High-yield spreads sit at 2.70%, well under the 350bps threshold for a benign environment, and IG at 0.80%. Both the St. Louis (^-0.79) and Chicago NFCI (^-0.56) stress indices indicate below-average stress, and bank lending standards are at a neutral 0.0 (no net tightening). The result: credit is available and cheap relative to earnings risk, supporting leverage but telling us the macro bond is inflation not default risk — this is a rate shock, not a credit shock.

### 6. HOUSING
The 30Y fixed mortgage rate jumped to 6.76% (15Y at 6.09%), a headwind off the Fed's hold. Existing home sales of 3.98M SAAR are slow, but new home sales (607K) and permits (1.43M) show builders still active; Case-Shiller is up only +2.1% YoY, meaning affordability is being restored by price stagnation rather than rate relief.

### 7. CONSUMER SENTIMENT
The U. Michigan sentiment index has deteriorated to 54.7 with a deteriorating six-month trend, and its 1-year inflation expectation jumped to 4.2% — a frustrated consumer bracing for higher prices even as the labor market stays resilient. This is the tension: households feel the inflation wall even as their paychecks and jobs remain secure.

### 8. MARKET IMPLICATIONS
- Defensive/short-duration tilt: restrictive real rates (near 4.6% real 10Y) + re-accelerating inflation favor equities with pricing power behe and short-duration cash flows; compress for credit spread beta.
- Energy & commodity-sensitive: the Iran oil shock and +9.85% PPI commodity surge favor energy and materials; expect further margin wherever input costs are now embedded in pricing.
- Financials benefit from a steepening-curve + restrained default risk mix (2Y/10Y +39bps, benign HY) — the primary beneficiary of the current backdrop.
- Stay underweight rate-sensitive long-duration growth and housing-linked names under 6.76% mortgage and a Fed on hold; the consumer is resilient but sentiment is fraying — monitor retail exposure to the personal-nonessential categories.

### 9. WEEKLY FORECAST
Expect the Fed to remain on hold at ~3.63% effective through the September meeting with a clear "wait-and-see" bias, keeping a hawkish risk to the front end as long as headline CPI stays above ~3.5% and the Iran supply shock persists. Core PCE will likely remain stubbornly in the 3.3–3.5% zone into 4Q24 as PPI momentum feeds through, so expect breakevens to hold at the 2.4–2.5% top edge of the credibility band. I put recession probability over the next 4–8 weeks at a moderate ~25%: growth has slowed, but the labor market and credit conditions are still too healthy to signal an imminent contraction. The single most important release next week is the FOMC/September rate decision and Powell's guidance — the second is the upcoming CPI release, which will determine whether the re-acceleration mo is re-accelerating or stabilizing.
